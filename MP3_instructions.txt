Machine Problem 3: Optimizing Kiosk Placement Using Hill Climbing Algorithm


The mayor of City XYZ is planning to build a new park with five entrance gates. The park will have three kiosks that will sell food, water, and juice to visitors. Your task is to determine the optimal placement of these kiosks such that they are as centrally located as possible, minimizing the total distance from all five gates. This will ensure that visitors entering from any gate can easily access the kiosks.

To solve this problem, you will use the Hill Climbing algorithm, a local search optimization technique. The park is represented as a 2D grid, and the gates and kiosks are represented as coordinates on this grid. Your goal is to find the best locations for the three kiosks such that the total distance from all gates to their nearest kiosk is minimized.

Hill Climbing Algorithm:

    The Hill Climbing algorithm is an iterative optimization technique that starts with a random solution and tries to improve it by making small changes.
    In this case, the algorithm will:
    Start with a random placement of kiosks.
    For each kiosk, consider moving it to its neighboring cells (up, down, left, right) that are not occupied by gates or other kiosks.
    Move the kiosk to a neighboring cell if it improves the total distance (i.e., reduces the total Manhattan distance from all gates to their nearest kiosk).
    Repeat this process until no further improvement can be made or a maximum number of iterations is reached.
